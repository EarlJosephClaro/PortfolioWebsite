# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The people this site must win over are recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Earl for a full-time role, and prospective freelance or contract clients sizing him up for project work. They arrive with a screening mindset and limited time: they want to know quickly whether this developer is credible, capable, and worth a conversation.

A secondary audience of fellow developers and peers — plus colleagues and managers at Sun-Asterisk in a career-growth context — will also browse. They read the work more closely and judge craft, so the site should hold up to a technical eye without being built for them first.

The job to be done is the same across all of them: form an accurate, confident impression of Earl's experience and ability, and know how to reach him.

## Product Purpose

This is Earl Joseph A. Claro's personal portfolio and resume site. It exists to present his professional story — a Junior / AI-enhanced full-stack developer at Sun-Asterisk working across React, Next.js, and NestJS — in a way that reads as genuine and earns trust. Success is a visitor who reads through the experience and skills, comes away believing Earl delivers reliable, maintainable work, and reaches out or connects.

## Positioning

A reliable full-stack developer who ships clean, maintainable work and has the delivery record to prove it — early to AI-driven development, but grounded in fundamentals, code review, and team collaboration rather than hype.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: explore the experience and skills sections — the site's job is to get visitors to actually read the story of what Earl has built. Secondary fallback: connect on GitHub or LinkedIn, and reach out via the contact section, for visitors not ready to make a hiring or project decision on the spot.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: a dependable developer who delivers — echoing Earl's own mission, *"Keep Building Confidence and Keep Coding."*
- Belief ladder: (1) this is a real, working developer, not a template; (2) he has genuine full-stack experience across frontend, backend, and enterprise systems; (3) his work is reliable — the delivery metrics back it up; (4) he'd be a low-risk, easy-to-work-with hire or collaborator; (5) he's worth contacting.
- Proof on hand: the delivery metrics already in the resume data (90+ HRIS tasks delivered, ~85% PR approval within 1–2 rounds, ~80–85% first-time acceptance, zero critical production bugs). A professional photo to add a personal, human anchor. Testimonials or references from managers and colleagues to be added later; leave a place for them.

## Brand Personality

Warm and personal in feel, without becoming casual or unprofessional — the opposite of a cold corporate template. The developer behind it is reliable and thorough: someone who ships clean, maintainable work, communicates early, and lets a strong track record speak quietly rather than overselling. The voice is confident but grounded and humble, matching the "keep building confidence, keep coding" ethos.

## Anti-references

Explicitly avoid the generic AI-portfolio template — the blue-gradient hero, muted gray text on tinted near-white, and endless identical icon-heading-text card grids the current build leans on. Avoid the cold, stiff, buzzword-heavy corporate-SaaS register. Avoid clutter and density: no walls of text or cramped screens. Warmth and craft should come from typography, spacing, color, and genuine personal detail, not from more decoration.

## Design Principles

Show the track record, don't just claim it — lead with concrete work and real metrics over adjectives. Human over template — every choice should read as a specific person's site, not a theme anyone could have downloaded. Earn trust through restraint — clean, calm, and legible beats loud; warmth is carried by tone and detail, not effects. Respect the visitor's time — clear hierarchy and scannable structure so a busy recruiter gets the impression fast. Craft that survives a second look — hold up under both a five-second scan and a developer's close read.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text at ≥4.5:1 contrast (large text ≥3:1), full keyboard navigation, semantic HTML and landmarks, visible focus states, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for every animation. This directly fixes a current weak spot — muted gray text on tinted backgrounds that likely fails AA today.
